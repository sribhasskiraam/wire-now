At least 10 killed in Guinea in crackdown on gold mining

Politics📍 Balato, Siguiri, Guinea 🌍 · 2026-11-13 · 📰 Reported by 2 outlets

📌 What happened

At least 10 people were killed and 13 injured when protesters and security forces clashed in Balato, Siguiri region, over enforcement of a gold mining ban. Two pick‑up trucks and a security forces truck were burned, and a firearm and motorcycle were seized by protesters. Five foreign nationals were among the dead, and the cause of the violence is still under investigation.

📖 The full story

The Ministry of Territorial Administration said late on Thursday that clashes occurred on Wednesday when security forces were verifying the implementation of the artisanal gold mining ban. Protesters erected barricades blocking the patrol, leading to exchanges of gunfire, though it was unclear who opened fire. The injured included civilians and members of the security forces.

Guinea banned artisanal gold mining in Siguiri on November 2 because miners failed to comply with regulations, causing environmental damage such as farmland destruction and river pollution. The government said the restrictions aim to curb this damage, and judicial authorities are tasked with determining what triggered the violence and who is responsible. Over 1,200 people were arrested earlier this month, and the first mass trials have opened this week.

Neighbouring Mali reported that about 100,000 people arrived in Bamako and nearly 60,000 more in Kouremale after being forced out of Guinea when the mining ban was announced. Burkina Faso said it repatriated 45,406 of its citizens who left Guinea because of the ban. Artisanal gold mining supports more than 300,000 livelihoods in Guinea, according to the UN Environment Programme, and is driven by rising metal demand across West Africa.
